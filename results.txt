Why the first step does not depend on dt: every step ball is empty of the old cut and touches it at its
closest point. Inside the cut: the ball meets the plane only at that point, so no strip can enter it. On a
lateral edge (on a tet face): the ball's disk in the plane lies beyond that edge's line, outside the section,
so no strip can enter it. On the front edge: the disk is tangent to the front line from ahead, so every
strip, however thin, enters it. At a front corner the disk's centre lies in the corner's normal cone, and
the disk still contains the points of every strip next to the corner along the lateral edge, so these
balls do not depend on dt either. So the invalidated set depends on where the front is, not on how far it
moves, and a smaller dt only means more updates for the same front travel. The cut is the only absorbing
boundary (the tet faces reflect), so every point ahead of the front has its closest absorbing point on
the front edge, and its first ball is invalidated by any advance.
Cost: a tested walk is replayed from its seed, which costs about as much as walking it, so the update
costs about (fraction tested) x (full solve), and a little more here because long walks have large boxes
and are tested more often. t_update/t_full vs fraction tested (means over the steps):
   1/8: 0.77 vs 0.63; 1/16: 0.85 vs 0.72; 1/32: 0.87 vs 0.75; 1/64: 0.92 vs 0.77
verdict: the first step's re-walked fraction shrinks with the strip size: NO (the first step's counts are identical
   for every dt: the invalidated set does not depend on dt, as argued above)
verdict: the update is faster than the same-seed full re-solve at every strip size: yes (speed-up 1.1x to 1.3x)
